Same key,
same mark.Sound, printed in sand.

Each note shakes a steel plate. Sand runs off the parts that move and piles up on the lines that stay still. That figure is printed as the record's centre label — read the key before you hear it.

    STEP
    TYPEFACES

    Schibsted Grotesk — a grotesk drawn for a newspaper house: it carries the "printing press" side of the work (headline, label names). Weight 850 for the thesis, italic 400 for the claim underneath — hierarchy by weight and posture, not size alone.
    Victor Mono — every number the plate produces (Hz, mode, cents, match). Its cursive italic is used for lab-notebook annotations, so measured values and human notes read as two different hands.

    MAPPING RULES

    note f → λ = λ0 · f / 261.63 Hz → the plate rings in the mode whose eigenvalue λ is nearest (square λ = m² + n², λ0 = 50; round λ = j′ₙₛ², λ0 = 81; violin λ = (m/0.62)² + n², λ0 = 45).
    Square figure u = cos φ·[cos mπx cos nπy + cos nπx cos mπy] + sin φ·[… − …]; φ comes from the bow point (keys drive the corner notch, φ = 0).
    Round figure u = Jₙ(j′ r)·cos n(θ − θbow) (free-edge membrane modes).
    Violin = rectangle modes warped to the outline: a rough approximation.
    Sand density D → exp(−(u / w)²), relaxing at rate 6 · amplitude · resonance; w narrows 0.55 → 0.05 in ~1 s (sand gathering). No particles: the sand is one density field.
    Detune: resonance weight 1 / (1 + ((λt − λi) / γ)²) over the two nearest modes (0.004 · λ).

    SOUND

    Every strike is a bell whose partials are the plate's own eigenvalue ratios (square ×1, ×1.25, ×2.25, ×2.5, ×3.25, ×4; round and violin differ), so each plate has its own metal. Dragging bows the plate: band-passed friction noise on the same partials. Control mode swaps in a plain harmonic chime (×1, ×2, ×3).

    MOTION

    Sand formation ≈ 0.6–1.2 s · trace pulses 380 ms · disc 5.6 s per turn (0.7 s per slot) · strike decay 3.2 s / (1 + 0.5k) · damp choke 80 ms.

    HONESTY

    This is an idealised mathematical model, not a measured plate. Real free-edge plates have different mode shapes and frequencies; here one octave is scaled onto the plate's modes, and the keys drive an ideal transducer. The figures are exact solutions of the model, not photographs.
