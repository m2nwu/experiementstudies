P9 · Territory v2

Travel a record label like a metro map. A small listening model in this page keeps what you stay for, and lays it down as a seventh line: yours.

1

Travel

Each line is a record, each station an artist. Hover a station to hear how they voice the chord. Wheel or ← → moves the train; 1–6 change line.

2

Stay

Linger 1.2 s on a station, stop the train for 2.5 s, or listen to a record. Each one becomes a ticket in your notebook, and the style card learns from how long you stayed.

3

Ride Your Line

Every ticket adds a station to Your Line. At 3 it plays music composed from your tickets; at 7 it gains a bass; at 12 a sleeve; at 20 it runs express.

Sound starts when you board. Space starts or stops anything playing, M mutes everything. Nothing leaves this page: your notebook is kept on this device only, and Reset forgets it.

How the cover was set

The drawing is Your Line itself, station for station. The title is set at the line's own weight. The ten ticks on the right are your voicing weights, Marisol Kett first. The title takes its adjective from your favourite voicing and its noun from the record you rode most.

P9 · Territory v2 · Specification

The visitor draws the site. Now the site also listens back.

The Collector, honestly

  • A small listening model running in this page: weighted counts, averages and a first-order Markov chain. Not a neural network, not an LLM, no network calls.
  • An inspiration is a stay: hovering a station 1.2 s or more, stopping the train 2.5 s or more, a listen of 8 beats or more, or a drag that moves 4 parcels or more.
  • Weight of a ticket = kind × log₂(1 + seconds stayed, capped at 30). Listen 1.5, linger 1.0, part listen 0.9, stop 0.8, survey 0.6. A prior of 3 keeps the first ticket from deciding everything.
  • Learned: share of each of the ten voicings; share of each line; tension (from each voicing's colour and the land you held); tempo (the BPM of the lines you stayed on); swing (long stays lean back, 1.3:1 to 2.6:1); register and density (from the voicings, plus restless surveying).
  • Every change is reported in one line: what happened, what moved, by how much.

Your Line

  • Starts under the terminus and grows one station per ticket, 1.5 parcels at a time, only in the eight transit directions, turning toward the station that inspired it and never back over itself. Where you linger, it coils.
  • Drawn in ink, the only ink line on the map, at the mean weight of the lines you collected from. Under 3 tickets it is dashed: under construction.
  • It has right of way: sections never sit on it, so the page rearranges as it grows. Each ticket also surveys 4 parcels along it, more the longer you stayed.
  • Music: chords from a Markov chain trained on the progressions of the lines you favour plus the order in which you collected chords; one bar per station, voiced by that station's artist, sometimes swapped for the voicing you favour. Tempo, swing, tension, register and comping density come from the style card.
  • 3 tickets: piano alone. 7: walking bass. 12: brushes and ride, and a sleeve. 20: a second chorus in the key of your second favourite line.

Typeface

Epilogue, one variable family, weight axis 100–900, nothing else. Lines are separated by mass, not colour. On the style card an artist's name is set heavier the more you have stayed with them, so preference is read as weight.

Land rules (from v1)

  • A fresh visitor holds one terminus. Listening earns parcels beat by beat along the line you hear; a full listen = the record's BPM in parcels.
  • Sections only appear where a whole rectangle of your land is free.
  • Dragging annexes a parcel and cedes your most remote one. Area is conserved.
  • Land beyond the terminus sets tension of the six records: 7th, 9th, 11th, 13th.

Sound

  • FM electric piano, triangle walking bass, noise brushes and ride. All synthesised; no files.
  • Nothing loops on its own. A record plays 32 beats; Your Line plays once through. Space stops either. M mutes everything. A compressor and a limiter sit before the output.

Colour

Paper #FFFFFF, ochre #C8961A, ink #1A1407. Unclaimed network is ochre on white; held land is white line on ochre; Your Line is ink on both.
